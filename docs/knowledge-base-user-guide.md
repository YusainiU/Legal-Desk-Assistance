# Legal Desk Assistance — Knowledge Base & Case References User Guide

**Audience:** New users, legal practitioners, and administrators  
**Applies to:** Legal Desk Assistance, law-resources package, November 2026  
**Purpose:** Explain how to research, record, review, retrieve, and reuse legal knowledge across cases.

> **Important:** This is an application user guide, not legal advice. A saved reference, imported judgment, or AI answer is not proof that an authority is authentic, current, or applicable. Always consult the primary source and check subsequent treatment.

## 1. Understand the workflow

Legal Desk Assistance has two connected work areas:

- **Case Register:** Your working matters. Each case contains a description, workflow stages, Legal References, a Final Decision, and a case history.
- **Library / Knowledge Base:** Your reusable research collection, with **AI Research**, **Case Judgements**, and **Case References** tabs.

A typical workflow is: open a case → identify legal issues → find or create authorities → explain their relevance in **Legal References** → document the outcome in **Final Decision** → optionally publish that decision to the Library → reuse appropriate references in a *different* open case.

A case-specific reference and an indexed court judgment are different records. The judgment stores the source material; the case reference records **why that material matters to a particular case**.

## 2. Open the Library

Use **Library** from the application's navigation, or select **Build Resources → Library**. The Library is divided into three tabs:

| Tab | What you will find | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Research** | AI search results you explicitly saved | Revisit a preliminary research answer |
| **Case Judgements** | Imported, indexed court judgments | Inspect an authority and its passages |
| **Case References** | Legal references recorded against your cases | Find previous reasoning and reuse it in another matter |

The Library's search box filters the relevant records. Case References can also be filtered by reference type. Search terms can match a reference's title, citation, jurisdiction, or explanation of applicability.

**Note:** The Library does not automatically verify legal sources or convert every research result into a case reference.

## 3. Start with a case

1. Open **Case Register**.
2. Select the case title to open its details.
3. Review the case description, parties, current stage, and status.
4. Open the **Legal References** tab to record relevant authorities.
5. Use **Final Decision** when you are ready to record the case outcome.

In the Case Register, the **Summary** link beside **Stage / Status** opens a read-only case summary. The same summary is accessible from an eligible source case in **Library → Case References**. It includes case information, history, and a formatted Final Decision where present.

## 4. Add a legal reference to a case

In an **Open** case, select **Legal References → Add reference**. Complete the fields:

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| **Reference type** | Statute, statutory provision, constitutional provision, legal or contract clause, case precedent, regulation/rule, or other authority |
| **Title or name** | A recognizable authority title |
| **Citation, section or clause** | The official citation or precise provision, if available |
| **Jurisdiction or court** | The relevant jurisdiction or issuing court |
| **Source URL** | A trustworthy original source link, when available |
| **Why it applies to this case** | A concrete explanation of the rule, relevant facts, and its effect on the case |

**Example:** Type: *Statutory provision*; Title: *Contracts Act 1950*; Citation: *Section [verified section]*; Why it applies: *Explain the specific issue, relevant facts, and how the verified provision bears on the client's position.* Do not invent section numbers or citations.

Select **Save reference**. You can create a new reference directly from the case; it does **not** need to exist in the Library first.

For an open case, use **Edit** to correct a reference or **Delete** to remove it. These actions are unavailable when the case is no longer open.

### Writing a useful applicability note

Explain (1) the legal question, (2) the precise proposition or provision, (3) the material case facts, (4) how the authority affects your analysis, and (5) any uncertainty, distinction, or later treatment. Avoid vague entries such as “Relevant to this case.”

## 5. Find references from earlier cases

1. Open **Library → Case References**.
2. Search by title, citation, jurisdiction, or the text of the applicability note.
3. Optionally narrow results by reference type.
4. Read the reference and identify its **source case**.
5. Select the case-summary link to inspect the original context, including the Final Decision and case history where available.

The summary helps you assess *why* the authority was used. Similar case names or facts do not establish that the same legal conclusion applies.

## 6. Copy a reference to another case

In **Library → Case References**, choose **Copy to case** for the desired reference.

1. Choose a **different Open case** from your Case Register.
2. Enter a **new explanation of why the reference applies to the target case**.
3. Confirm the copy.
4. Open the target case's **Legal References** tab to review the new entry.

The application copies the reference's type, title, citation, jurisdiction, and source URL, but asks for a fresh applicability explanation. It creates a separate reference in the destination case; it does not move or delete the source reference.

You cannot copy a reference back into its own source case through this workflow, or select a closed target case. Verify the authority again before relying on it.

## 7. Build Resources: Court Judgement Builder

Open **Build Resources → Court Judgement Builder** to inspect judgments indexed in the application.

The **Created judgments** list shows imported judgments, newest first. Select a judgment title to open its details. Depending on the imported source, the record may include the case title, court, citation, judgment date, source URL, and indexed passages.

### Import a judgment (administrator)

Users with the required administrator permission can use **Administrator: import judgment text**:

1. Enter the case title and select the court.
2. Optionally enter the reported citation, judgment date, and source URL.
3. Paste judgment text from a source you are authorised to store.
4. Confirm that you have permission to index and use the text.
5. Select **Import as unverified source**.
6. Inspect the resulting judgment in **Created judgments** or **Library → Case Judgements**.

An imported judgment is initially **unverified**. The application does not certify that the text is complete, accurately transcribed, or still good law.

## 8. Research indexed judgments

The internal research functionality can search indexed passages for a legal research question and optional court preference. Results depend on which judgments have been imported and indexed. If there are no matching passages, add authorised judgments before expecting results.

Where available, you can inspect an indexed judgment, open the original source, generate an **optional AI draft** based on selected passages, and add a research authority to an **Open case's Legal References**.

When an authority is added to a case from research, the application creates a case-reference record with a research note. **Review and improve its applicability explanation** before using it in a legal document.

The underlying internal research functionality and the Court Judgement Builder's currently visible controls are not identical; not every internal action is necessarily exposed on the Builder's main screen.

## 9. Search AI Resources

Open **Build Resources → Search AI resources**.

1. Write a focused **Legal research question**.
2. Choose a **Court preference** (all courts, Federal Court, Court of Appeal, or High Court).
3. Optionally select **Link to case**; choose **Personal research** if it is not for a particular matter.
4. Select **Search AI Resources**.
5. Review the result and its source claims carefully.
6. Select **Save to Knowledge Base** if the answer is useful enough to retain.

AI search results are **unverified preliminary research**, even when they contain citations. Check original decisions, quoted paragraphs, dates, jurisdiction, and later treatment independently.

**Link to case** associates the research record with an open matter; it does not automatically create a legal reference in that matter. If the research identifies a useful authority, record it in the case's Legal References with a case-specific explanation.

### Find and remove saved AI research

- **Recent AI searches** lets you reopen recent results.
- **Library → AI Research** displays results explicitly saved to the knowledge base.
- The Library offers an action to remove a saved AI research entry. Removing a saved entry is distinct from editing a case reference.

Do not enter confidential client information into external AI research without appropriate authorisation.

## 10. Record the Final Decision

Open a case and select **Final Decision**.

1. Enter the decision using the rich-text editor.
2. Use formatting such as headings, bold, italic, lists, and tables to make the reasoning readable.
3. Select **Save/Update**.
4. Reopen the tab if needed to confirm the saved formatting and content.

A Final Decision is required before the case can be closed. The editor stores formatted HTML; the application sanitizes saved HTML and imposes a maximum saved length. Image-insertion toolbar controls are hidden in this editor.

**Saving the Final Decision does not automatically publish it to the Library.**

### Publish the Final Decision

Select **Add to Knowledge Base** from the Final Decision tab after saving. This creates or updates a special **Case Reference** in the Library with a title based on the source case. Subsequent edits to a published Final Decision update the linked Library entry.

The source case's published Final Decision is intentionally **excluded from that same case's Legal References tab** to avoid a duplicate self-reference. It remains visible in **Library → Case References** and in the case summary.

## 11. Read case summaries

You can open a case summary in two ways:

- **Case Register:** Select **Summary** next to the case's Stage / Status.
- **Library → Case References:** Use the source-case summary link.

The summary is a read-only overview, not the editing screen. The Final Decision is rendered as formatted, sanitized HTML rather than showing HTML tags. Use the case detail view to make permitted changes.

## 12. Understand what is shared and what is separate

| Action | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Create a reference inside a case | Creates a case-specific reference; it appears in the Library's Case References |
| Copy a reference to another case | Creates a new reference in the target case with a new applicability note |
| Import a judgment | Creates an indexed judgment, not automatically a case reference |
| Run AI search | Creates an unverified research result |
| Save AI research to Knowledge Base | Makes that result available in the AI Research Library |
| Link AI search to a case | Associates research with the case, without automatically adding a legal reference |
| Save Final Decision | Updates the case record |
| Add Final Decision to Knowledge Base | Publishes or updates a linked Library reference |
| Close a case | Requires a saved Final Decision; editing case references is restricted afterward |

## 13. Example: working from first instruction to closure

**Scenario:** A client asks about a disputed contract termination.

1. Create the case and describe the dispute, dates, parties, and questions.
2. Search the Library for previous contract-termination references.
3. Open the source-case summary to understand the earlier facts and outcome.
4. If useful, copy an authority into the new case and write a new applicability explanation.
5. Research additional judgments in Court Judgement Builder and, if enabled, AI Resources.
6. Verify primary legislation, case citations, relevant paragraphs, and later judicial treatment.
7. Add or correct the case's Legal References as your analysis develops.
8. Record the final conclusion and reasons in **Final Decision**; save it.
9. Optionally select **Add to Knowledge Base** so the outcome can inform future work.
10. Complete the remaining case workflow and close the case once all closure requirements are met.

## 14. Good knowledge-management practice

Use descriptive titles and precise citations. Record the jurisdiction and original source. Distinguish binding authority, persuasive authority, contractual terms, and factual background. Record the *reasoning* that connects an authority to a matter, not merely its name.

Review changes in law, amendments, appeals, reversals, and distinguishing decisions. Do not treat an old case's final outcome as a precedent in itself. Apply appropriate confidentiality and access controls when documenting client facts and decisions.

## 15. Permissions and limitations

- Case and case-reference access in these screens is scoped to the signed-in user's cases.
- Copy destinations must be **Open** cases owned by that user, excluding the source case.
- Adding, editing, and deleting a case reference requires the case to be **Open**.
- Judgment import is restricted to users with the required administrative permission.
- Administrators can enable or disable AI Search and configure its research jurisdiction; disabled search does not erase previously saved research.
- AI features depend on configured providers and may be unavailable or fail independently of the Library.
- The law-resources package is undergoing extraction into a standalone package; some research functionality is available in the underlying code but not directly surfaced in the current screens.

## 16. Troubleshooting

**A reference is not in the Library.** Confirm it was saved to the intended case. Clear Library search/type filters. Remember that AI search results require **Save to Knowledge Base** to appear under AI Research.

**Cannot copy a reference.** Check that the target is a different **Open** case in your Case Register, and supply the required target-case applicability note.

**Cannot edit a reference.** Check whether the case is closed or declined.

**A Final Decision does not appear in Case References.** Save the decision and explicitly choose **Add to Knowledge Base**. Its absence from the originating case's Legal References tab is intentional.

**Formatting looks wrong in a summary.** Ensure the application assets and views are up to date. The summary renders sanitized HTML with the application's legal rich-text styles.

**AI Search is disabled or fails.** Ask an administrator to check AI Search settings, provider configuration, and application logs. Existing saved research remains available when new searches are disabled.

**No judgments found.** The indexed collection may be empty or lack relevant passages; an authorised administrator can import judgment text.
